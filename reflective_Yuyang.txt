1. I think current system doesn't provide adequate accountability for the whole rentals in San Francisco. The main problem is that the self-reporting system is not so reliable. Hosts are required to register and follow rules, but many listings don't follow proper registration process and may misrepresent status. Platforms like Airbnb are expected to enforce compliance, but they are more likely to maximize listings and revenue instead. This is conflicted with what people expect from the enforcement. 
2. One factor is whether one is authorized when doing web scraping. For example, scraping some kinds of public data may be allowed, but repetitive scraping after being blocked or violating access restrictions can lead to legal liability. Therefore, it is how access is obtained that we need to evaluate when we are discussing whether the scraping is legal or not. This is essential for us to tell unlawful intrusion from normal browsing by the public.
3. I would avoid access those private information of users even though the information is open to the public. As users, we know that when we make some information open to public, it doesn't always mean that we want them to be colleceted, repurposed, etc. Therefore, we should also respect others as if we want others respect our sensitive information. 
Also, when we are doing scraping, we should always supervise ourselves. Whenever we do scraping, we need to ask ourselves whether we are using those data in an ethical way, in a way that beneficial for the society. If the use case could mislead, discriminate, or harm people like in cases such as Facebook–Cambridge Analytica data scandal, then scraping should not be performed.
